# CAP182-SS1-STADIOEquities
CAP182 SS1 Capstone Project for STADIOEquities – Predicting Client Dormancy and Identifying Investors at Risk of Becoming Inactive.

# PART A: MOTIVATION
STADIOEquities is a South African digital investment platform designed to make investing accessible to a broad population, including first-time investors. The company has approximately 2.3 million registered accounts and 760,000 funded and active accounts, while 41% of registered accounts have never been funded. The platform employs approximately 700 people across product, engineering, client service and compliance. This large customer base creates significant opportunities for growth, but it also creates a central business challenge: acquiring an account is relatively inexpensive, while converting that account into a funded, active and long-term investor is where the business derives most of its value.
The activation and engagement problem is financially important because STADIOEquities earns revenue from activities and balances associated with funded clients. Its revenue streams include platform and administration fees, brokerage and transaction fees, interest margin on uninvested client cash, premium subscriptions and value-added products. Almost every revenue stream depends on clients funding their accounts and continuing to use the platform. An empty account therefore provides limited economic value, while a client who becomes inactive can represent lost future activity and revenue.
The briefing pack shows that the activation funnel is weakening. Sign-up to first-deposit conversion has declined from 64% two years ago to 59%, while the proportion of accounts becoming dormant within six months has increased from 22% to 31%. KYC onboarding abandonment has also increased from 13% to 18%. These trends suggest that STADIOEquities is not only facing difficulty converting registrations into funded accounts, but is also experiencing a growing problem of clients becoming inactive after joining the platform.
The problem is made more significant by the fact that STADIOEquities already has extensive data about the client journey. The platform records app and web behaviour, onboarding steps, deposits, withdrawals, balances, trades, instruments held, trading frequency, product adoption, client demographics, support interactions and marketing engagement. The company therefore has the raw information needed to investigate patterns that may precede dormancy. However, the information pack identifies the business as “data rich, insight poor”, because behavioural, trading, funding and support information is rarely joined into one view of a client.
A data science project focused on predicting customer dormancy can add practical value by identifying patterns that occur before an account becomes inactive. Rather than waiting until a client has already become dormant, STADIOEquities could use historical information to identify clients whose behaviour resembles that of previously dormant clients. This could allow the business to prioritise engagement actions at an earlier stage and use its limited marketing and customer-service resources more effectively.
The project is also directly aligned with the STADIOEquities 2030 strategy. One of the board's priorities is to “activate the accounts we already have” by identifying clients who are likely to activate or stall and intervening at the right moment. Another priority is to keep clients engaged for the long run by seeing dormancy coming before an account goes quiet and winning back clients who are drifting away. A dormancy prediction project therefore addresses two explicit strategic priorities rather than applying data science to an unrelated business issue.
The project could benefit several stakeholders. Customer-retention and marketing teams could use risk information to prioritise engagement campaigns. Product teams could investigate behaviours associated with disengagement and improve the client experience. Customer-service teams could identify clients who may require assistance before inactivity becomes established. Senior management could gain a clearer view of the factors associated with long-term client value and use evidence to guide activation and retention strategies.
The project is therefore motivated by a measurable business problem, an available data foundation and a clear strategic need. STADIOEquities has millions of registered accounts but a substantially smaller active base, while dormancy has increased to 31%. Developing a predictive approach to identify likely dormancy can help move the business from reacting to inactivity toward anticipating it. This provides a focused, industry-related Data Science problem with potential operational and strategic value.

# PART B: PROBLEM STATEMENT
STADIOEquities has a large registered customer base, but a significant proportion of these accounts do not become active long-term investment relationships. The information pack reports 2.3 million registered accounts compared with 760,000 funded and active accounts, and 41% of registered accounts have never deposited funds. More importantly, the percentage of accounts that become dormant within six months has increased from 22% two years ago to 31% currently. At the same time, sign-up to first-deposit conversion has fallen from 64% to 59%, indicating a weakening activation funnel.
This problem affects STADIOEquities because the company's economic model depends heavily on clients funding their accounts and remaining engaged. Platform and administration fees depend on assets under administration, brokerage revenue depends on trading activity, interest margin depends on client cash balances, and premium and value-added products depend on client adoption and retention. When clients remain unfunded or become dormant, the platform has limited opportunity to generate these forms of recurring value.
The problem also affects teams responsible for growth, marketing, customer retention, product development and customer service. Current business information indicates that onboarding emails and nudges are sent on fixed schedules rather than being tailored to whether a client is likely to activate or disappear. In addition, the client base is highly varied, including different investing behaviours, yet clients receive a largely generic experience. This limits the organisation's ability to respond appropriately to individual patterns of disengagement.
What is not sufficiently understood is which client characteristics and behaviours observable before inactivity are associated with a higher likelihood of subsequent dormancy. Although STADIOEquities collects app and web behaviour, account and funding information, trading activity, product and subscription data, demographics, support interactions and marketing engagement, the briefing pack states that these data sources are rarely joined into one client view.
The proposed Data Science project will therefore investigate whether historical client data available before a defined prediction point can be used to identify clients who are likely to become dormant within six months. The study will examine relationships between onboarding, funding, digital engagement, trading, products, client profiles, support and marketing engagement and the subsequent dormancy outcome. The objective is to provide a defensible data-driven basis for earlier and more targeted client-engagement decisions.
